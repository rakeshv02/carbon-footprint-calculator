Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average carbon footprint per person?

Global average: ~4 tonnes CO₂/year. UK average: ~5.5 tonnes. US average: ~14 tonnes. Australia: ~15 tonnes. To keep global warming below 1.5°C, the target is around 2.5 tonnes per person by 2030.

What has the biggest impact on my carbon footprint?

For most people: flights (a single long-haul return flight can add 1.5–3 tonnes), diet (meat-heavy diets produce 2× more than vegan), and home heating (gas boilers are a major source in the UK). Switching to an EV and heat pump makes a large dent.

How can I offset my carbon footprint?

You can offset through certified schemes (Gold Standard, Verified Carbon Standard). However, most climate scientists advise reducing first, then offsetting the unavoidable remainder. Offsets should not replace behavioural change.

How accurate is this calculator?

This provides a reasonable estimate based on average emissions factors. Real footprints vary by your local energy grid's carbon intensity, specific vehicle efficiency, food sourcing, and more. It's a useful starting point for identifying the biggest areas to target.

Does recycling significantly reduce my footprint?

Recycling helps but has a relatively small impact compared to diet, flights, and heating. The biggest individual actions are: eating less meat, flying less, switching to an EV, and installing a heat pump or solar panels.

What is the Carbon Footprint Calculator?

A carbon footprint calculator estimates the greenhouse gas emissions caused by your lifestyle — travel, home energy, diet and consumption — expressed in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent per year. This free carbon footprint calculator helps you see where your biggest emissions come from and where cuts matter most.

How it works

Each activity is multiplied by an emission factor (kg CO₂e per unit) and summed: total = Σ (activity × emission factor). For example, petrol produces roughly 2.3 kg CO₂ per litre, and grid electricity varies by country (often 0.2–0.5 kg CO₂e per kWh).

Adding up transport, home energy, food and goods gives an annual total you can compare against national averages and climate targets.

Car commuteDriving 15,000 km a year in a petrol car using ~1,200 litres of fuel produces about 1,200 × 2.3 ≈ 2.76 tonnes of CO₂ from driving alone.
A long-haul flightA single long-haul return flight can add roughly 1.5–3 tonnes of CO₂e — often more than a person's entire year of home electricity.

When to use it

  • Understanding which parts of your life emit the most.
  • Setting personal reduction goals against a target.
  • Comparing the impact of driving, flying or diet changes.
  • Deciding how much to offset after cutting what you can.
